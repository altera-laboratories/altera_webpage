Personality first.

Round three. The palette rounds applied color to one identical wireframe; the verdict was that everything felt the same. These seven concepts are the opposite experiment: each designer built a world, with its own layout, texture, type voice, and annotations, then expressed it in a light mode and a dark mode. Same product, same facts, seven personalities.

1 · Marginalia

The whole stack, graded in red pencil

Every instructor already trusts one interface: a marked-up page handed back with red pencil in the margins. Marginalia makes the product feel like that page, charcoal ink on warm gray paper, one carmine pencil doing the circling, the squiggles, and the terse verdicts like "see me". The voice is a professor at 11pm finally getting help with the stack, so the mastery model reads as annotation you would have written yourself, not a dashboard.

2 · Chalk & Pine

The same classroom, first bell to lights out

Every other edtech dark mode is just inverted grays, but a classroom already owns the best dark surface ever made: the blackboard. Day is a field guide on ruled lesson-plan paper with specimen labels, circled observations, and a rotated grant stamp; night is the identical room after hours, drawn in chalk with wobbly dashed borders, density-coded chalk-stroke mastery bars, a wooden frame with a real chalk tray, and one honest eraser smudge where someone rubbed out "quiz friday??". The two mocks argue that Altera lives where teaching actually happens, and the mastery ranking stays colorblind-safe because rank is carried by position, glyph fill, and mark density, never hue.

3 · Meridian

The observatory for what students know

Meridian treats teaching as astronomy: by day the site is a university press page, engraved rules, small caps, a gold seal, and by night the same class becomes a star chart where each student is a star whose magnitude is their mastery. The metaphor is load bearing, not decorative: brightness, altitude, and mark shape encode the ranking, so the product's core promise, seeing, is literally the visual system. The voice stays precise and calibrated, like an instrument log, which matches an audience of instructors who trust measurement.

4 · Verdigris

Mastery you can read like an instrument

The product promise is precise live measurement, so the whole site becomes a beautiful instrument: etched dial gauges whose needles sit at real student readings, copper patina hairlines, stamped unit plates, and terse engineering microcopy that treats teaching data with the seriousness of a ship's log. Day mode reads as a lab manual on cream paper with graph-grid and engraved rules; night mode is the same instrument glowing soft verdigris phosphor in a petrol-dark wheelhouse. Ranking stays colorblind-safe because every gauge encodes level three ways at once: needle position, band length and lightness, and a plain-language status word with a rotated re-teach stamp on the struggling student.

5 · The Blue Book

The exam book that finally gets graded back

Every instructor and student already has muscle memory for this object: the speckled cover, the ruled page, the red pen in the margin. Altera flips its meaning, the exam book now reports back to the teacher, with Name: your students and Date: before the next class doing the pitch in six words. The world is built from real school artifacts (marbled covers, index tabs, a circled 41%, a TEDCO rubber stamp, a graded A on the professor quote) so warmth and rigor arrive together, Wes Anderson restraint rather than clip art nostalgia.

7 · The Observatory

Every class is a night sky.

Instructors already talk like astronomers: bright students, faint signals, kids who drift off the chart. The Observatory makes that metaphor the whole interface, so the mastery model becomes a live sky survey with a re-teach horizon, signal-strength glyphs, a field-log voice, and a night watchman's hand scribbling where to point the telescope on Thursday. It earns a real light and dark pair for free: day shift in the chart room, night shift with the dome open, same instruments, same log.
