Four color schemes, same page.

Each option keeps the Reading Room's editorial structure (Fraunces, paper cards, mastery semantics: green high, amber medium, rose low) and moves the neutral temperature plus the accent family away from the cream-and-terracotta pairing. Each follows a named scheme from the Week 10 lecture, the 60-30-10 rule, and desaturated tones.

Mastery colors and royal-blue-for-data are held constant in every option; only brand neutrals and the accent change.

1 · Heritage

Monochromatic navy + gold complement for CTAs

Oxford-navy ink on cool ivory, the university-press look taken toward JHU's own heritage blue. The lecture's "monochromatic with a complement for calls to action": everything is navy tints except the one gold CTA. Reads institutional and trustworthy; zero Claude.

2 · Field Notes

Analogous greens (pine · moss · eggshell)

The naturalist's notebook: analogous greens from the lecture's "harmony, found in nature" family. Pine for actions, moss tints for warmth, eggshell paper. Green = growth is the most native metaphor education has. Accent pine is far darker than the emerald mastery-high, so the semantics stay legible.

3 · Letterpress

Near-monochromatic aubergine on warm porcelain

A scholarly plum: aubergine ink on porcelain-gray paper, the rare-books room rather than the startup deck. Distinctive (almost no edtech uses violet family), quietly premium, and maximally far from both Claude's coral and the competitors' blues. The lecture's "fewer colors" guideline taken furthest.

4 · Harbor

Analogous blues (marine · slate · glacier)

The Field Notes recipe transposed to blue, per Alex: deep marine for actions, slate-blue body text, glacier-tinted paper. Same analogous harmony, colder register: reads calm, technical, institutional. The marine CTA sits far darker than the royal data-blue, so measurements still pop against the brand color the way pine sat apart from the mastery emerald.
